Google Backstops a $19B Lease While Ohio Voters Draw a 25 MW Line

By Andi | Market Intelligence Analyst · NOVEMBER 10, 2026 · 7 min read
Share Post LinkedIn Email

The scarce input in this market is no longer megawatts. It is a credit rating you can put in front of a lender, and a zoning posture that survives the next election. This week produced one of each, plus a Wyoming site that shows what happens when a developer runs out of the first one.

Google lent its balance sheet to an AI lab in Kentucky and bought a stalled neocloud site in Wyoming. Lambda named itself the end user of a campus in Alabama that is already in court. Amazon confirmed what it plans to build on port land in Washington. And voters in Ohio wrote a 25 MW ceiling into several city charters. Four deals and one ballot result, none of them cheap, all of them conditional on something other than capital.

Google Co-Signs Anthropic's Kentucky Lease

On November 4, TeraWulf said Google signed three credit support agreements behind Anthropic's lease at the Justified campus in Hawesville, Kentucky. The lease runs 20 years on roughly 401 MW of critical IT load, with about $19B in contracted revenue. The same day TeraWulf executed a definitive EPC agreement with Fluor for three buildings. Delivery is phased from late 2027 through the second quarter of 2028.

The mechanics matter more than the headline. Each backstop takes effect when the final data hall under its lease commences. If Anthropic defaults, Google can step into the lease or decline it and pay a termination fee, though reporting on the filing says the termination route is restricted in the first six years unless the installed TPU pods can use less than half of tenant critical power. This is a hyperscaler guaranteeing a tenant that cannot yet carry its own paper, and the guarantee exists for one reason: so project lenders will write the debt.

Run the lease as a rent roll and it works out to about $47M per MW over the term. Whether that is rich depends on who you believe is on the other side of the check, which is exactly the question the backstop answers. For brokers pricing comparable powered shells, the credit enhancement is now a negotiated line item, not a favor.

Deal specs. Sponsor: TeraWulf (landlord), Anthropic (tenant), Google (credit support) · Site: Hawesville, Hancock County, KY, miles to nearest city limits n/d · Footprint: three buildings, sf n/d · Load: 401 MW critical IT (density n/d) · Lease: 20-yr with Google backstop, escalator n/d, tenant unrated · Source: DCD.

Google Buys Crusoe's Wyoming Site and Brings Its Own Fuel

On November 5, Google announced a $17B campus at the Switchgrass Industrial Park in Laramie County, about eight miles south of Cheyenne. The site was Crusoe's paused "Project Jade." Google took it over through a subsidiary, Jupiter Star Holdings, and calls it Project Tembo. It sits on 716 acres, and secondary reports put the build at about 3.08M sf across four main buildings.

The power story is the real news. A Bloom Energy fuel cell fleet of 702 MW, with carbon capture, will be owned and operated through an AEP vehicle, and Black Hills is separately investing $1.8B from 2027 to 2029 in 564 MW of gas generation. Google has not disclosed the campus load, and figures north of 2 GW circulating in the press are not the same thing as the fuel cell supply. Full completion is targeted for 2031.

Read it as a template. A neocloud assembles land and an interconnect path, the market turns, and a hyperscaler with a bigger balance sheet absorbs the position. If you hold entitled land with a power path, your exit may not be a tenant. It may be the buyer's own development team, arriving with its own generation. The community benefits package is still described locally as vague, which is where the next fight will start.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Google (owner-user) via Jupiter Star Holdings, predecessor Crusoe · Site: Switchgrass Industrial Park, Laramie County, WY, about 8 mi south of Cheyenne, 716 acres · Footprint: about 3.08M sf, four buildings (single source) · Load: n/d, with 702 MW of on-site fuel cell supply · Lease: owner-occupied, no lease · Source: Google.

Lambda Anchors an Alabama Campus With a Lawsuit Attached

Lambda announced it will invest an estimated $33.1B in Project Red Clay, a 2,200-acre campus in northeastern Lowndes County, Alabama, near U.S. 80 and Alabama 21, about six miles southwest of Montgomery. Cloverleaf Infrastructure is the developer and Lambda is the end user. The base case is 1,000 MW with room to grow to 1,500 MW. Construction starts in the first quarter of 2027 and runs up to five years, and power from Alabama Power still needs regulatory approval.

The entitlement package is the exposure. A second lawsuit, filed by Protect Lowndes County and the Alabama Rivers Alliance with SELC and NRDC as counsel, challenges the October 13 tax abatement approval. An earlier suit came from two residents on October 18. What is not in dispute is that the tax package is now litigation, and a lender underwriting a multi-year build will price that.

The tenant structure is also thin. Lambda is described as an end user, with no disclosed lease term, escalator or credit support. Compare that with Kentucky, where the credit was engineered before the debt was raised. In Alabama the sequence is reversed: the headline number came first, and the paper that makes it financeable has not been shown.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Cloverleaf Infrastructure (developer), Lambda (end user) · Site: northeastern Lowndes County, AL, about 6 mi SW of Montgomery, 2,200 acres · Footprint: n/d · Load: 1,000 MW base, up to 1,500 MW (density n/d) · Lease: n/d, tenant credit n/d · Source: Alabama Reporter.

Amazon Puts a Number on Walla Walla

Amazon Web Services detailed a campus at the Wallula Gap Business Park near Attalia, in Walla Walla County, Washington: three buildings of 220,000 sf each and about 300 MW. The Port of Walla Walla sold 554 acres for $36M in February, and the buildings occupy 134 of them. This replaces an earlier plan of 16 buildings at $4.8B, so the project got smaller before it got approved.

The density is the interesting spec. Three buildings at 660,000 sf total and 300 MW works out to roughly 455 W/sf, well above the 150 to 250 W/sf typical of a conventional cloud hall. The source does not say whether the 300 MW is IT load or total facility power, so the true IT density could be lower.

The gating item is state environmental review. The first comment period closed in mid-October, and a second opens after county review finishes. A utility still has to complete a substation pad. Amazon owns the dirt outright, so there is no landlord to sign anything. The risk is schedule, not credit.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Amazon Web Services (owner-user) · Site: Wallula Gap Business Park, Attalia, Walla Walla County, WA, distance to city limits n/d · Footprint: three 220 ksf buildings, about 660 ksf total on 134 acres · Load: about 300 MW (about 455 W/sf computed, IT vs total n/d) · Lease: owner-occupied, land bought for $36.0M · Source: Baxtel.

Ohio Voters Write a 25 MW Line Into Charters

Not a deal, but it reprices every site in the state. On November 3, voters in Trenton approved Issue 17 with about 80 percent, prohibiting data centers above 25 MW. Oregon, in Lucas County, passed the same threshold with 61 percent, and Piqua passed it with about 67 percent. Several smaller communities followed, with Defiance, Sunbury, Pataskala and Conneaut all clearing the same kind of ceiling on unofficial counts. These are charter amendments with a size threshold, not blanket bans, although some outlets report them loosely as bans.

The Trenton result lands on real projects. It puts a 250 MW Prologis project in question and blocks a planned 1,000 MW annexation by Amazon. A 25 MW ceiling is deliberate: it allows the small inference and edge node and shuts out the campus.

For this newsletter's audience the result cuts both ways. Sub-25 MW sites in Ohio just became more defensible, and anything above that needs a different jurisdiction or a different pitch to the voters.

One Site, Still Due Diligence

A shorter note on Goochland County, Virginia. Richmond BizSense reported on October 28 that Google is evaluating about 1,000 acres at West Creek Business Park for a campus of up to 1.2 GW. The parcel sits in a technology overlay district adopted in November 2025, which makes a data center a by-right use with no public hearing. Google has filed no plans with the county and is described as conducting due diligence. By-right zoning is rare now, and it explains why a company that keeps facing hearings elsewhere is looking here.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Google (evaluating), land owner tied to Pruitt Associates and Riverstone Group · Site: West Creek Business Park, off Route 6, Goochland County, VA, about 1,000 acres · Footprint: n/d · Load: 1,200 MW (density n/d) · Lease: n/d, no filings yet · Source: Richmond BizSense.

What the Week Adds Up To

Line the four deals up and the pattern is structural. In Kentucky, a rated sponsor rents its credit to an unrated tenant. In Wyoming, a rated sponsor buys a position from a developer that could not finish. In Alabama, an unrated end user has a big number and a docket. In Washington, a rated owner builds on its own land and waits for permits. The common variable is who carries the risk until the power arrives.

The Ohio ballots add a second variable. Capital and megawatts can be bought. A local majority cannot, and more of them are voting. If you are sizing a site, the first question is whether the jurisdiction has already said how large is too large, and the second is whether the tenant's paper will survive a lender's read.

What to Watch Next Week

Effingham County, Georgia, November 17. Commissioners delayed the site plan vote for the OpenAI-linked Project Camellia, a 1,347-acre, six-building proposal, to this date.

The ING loan auction, bids due November 19. About $500M of data center loans across 12 positions are on offer, including a $25M piece tied to a Stargate site in New Mexico and exposure to Vantage and CyrusOne. The clearing price is a read on secondary debt appetite.

Hanover Township, Pennsylvania. Hearings on Prime Data Centers' seven-building, 350 MW gas-powered campus were scheduled for November 10 and 11, with a decision to follow.

Spartanburg County, South Carolina. The 457 MW, $2.8B Valara project passed second reading 3 to 2 and now faces a third.

Cochise County, Arizona, December 9. A 747-acre, 6 GW proposal reportedly linked to SpaceX draws its hearing, and pre-hearing positioning should start now.
