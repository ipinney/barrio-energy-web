Blackstone Pays a 7% Cap for AWS Shells, and QTS Buys Its Own Power

By Andi | Market Intelligence Analyst · NOVEMBER 17, 2026 · 7 min read
Share Post LinkedIn Email

Four of this week's deals have one thing in common, and it is not a tenant. It is who owns the power. A lease used to be square footage with a megawatt number stapled on. Now the power contract is the lease, the shell is the collateral, and the lender is the one asking when the substation energizes.

Blackstone's new REIT paid a 7.0% cap rate for a slice of Amazon's powered shells. A PPL and Blackstone joint venture in Pennsylvania agreed terms to build a 2 GW generation fleet for QTS. A developer pulled $1.5 billion of corporate debt to hold interconnection slots before its tenants move in. And a New England family business pitched the region's biggest campus into a town that has not zoned for one.

Blackstone's REIT Pays a 7% Cap for AWS Shells

The first acquisition for BXDC, the Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust, is a $5 billion purchase of a 75% interest in two joint ventures holding 13 newly built powered shells. AWS keeps the other 25% and leases back 100% of the space, triple net, on a 12-year initial term that stretches to 32 years if every extension is exercised. The 8-K exhibit puts the initial cap rate at 7.0%, with contractual escalators whose rate was not disclosed.

That 7.0% is the number to carry into your own models. Sale-leasebacks to a hyperscaler tenant have been priced in private for two years, and this is a public vehicle printing a comp with a closing date attached (expected in the fourth quarter, subject to conditions). A public REIT now has to defend that yield to its own shareholders, which tends to make a buyer less flexible on the next one.

What the filing does not say is where the buildings are. The press release, the 8-K and the trade coverage all skip the campus locations and the square footage, so there is no density to compute. I am not going to guess at a site. The fourth-quarter closing disclosure is where that gets answered, and a shell portfolio of this size will not stay anonymous for long once title work is recorded.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (buyer), AWS (seller, 25% retained) · Site: n/d, two JVs, 13 shells · Footprint: n/d · Load: n/d · Lease: 12-yr initial term, up to 32 yrs with extensions, triple net, 100% leased back to AWS, escalators n/d · Deal value: ~$5B for 75%, 7.0% initial cap rate · Source: IREI, SEC.

QTS Signs Up Its Own 2 GW Power Plant

In Salem Township, Luzerne County, Pennsylvania, QTS agreed key commercial terms on a letter of intent with Invitium Energy, a joint venture led by PPL (51%) with Blackstone Infrastructure as partner. The plan is two combined-cycle gas plants plus fuel cells and batteries, about 2 GW of generation, serving a QTS campus. PPL disclosed it on its third-quarter call, and Utility Dive carried the terms.

Fuel cells and batteries could arrive as early as 2029, and the gas plants in 2032 and 2033. The agreement is a non-binding letter of intent, and PPL and QTS are now working toward a long-form agreement. A generation fleet sold to a tenant as part of the campus deal is the clearest version yet of the bring-your-own-power lease: the landlord side takes the capital risk on the plants, and the tenant takes the schedule risk.

Note the sequencing. First power is three years out at the earliest and the heavy iron is six years out, so whatever the tenant pays, it commits long before it sees a kilowatt-hour. The payment terms, the escalator, the tenant's credit and the campus square footage are all undisclosed in what I could confirm. Treat the headline megawatts as generation capacity, not IT load, and do not compute a density from them.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Invitium Energy (PPL 51%, with Blackstone Infrastructure) supplying QTS Data Centers · Site: Salem Township, Luzerne County, PA, distance to nearest city limits n/d · Footprint: n/d · Load: ~2 GW of generation (IT load and W/sf n/d) · Lease: non-binding LOI, long-form agreement pending, term and payment structure n/d · Tenant credit: n/d · Source: Utility Dive.

Rowan Borrows $1.5B Before the Tenants Arrive

Rowan Digital Infrastructure closed more than $1.5 billion of corporate-level financing: a $600 million term loan upsize led by Apterra Infrastructure Capital, a $590 million revolver and a $355 million letter-of-credit facility, both led by Citibank. Mizuho, MUFG, Goldman Sachs Bank USA, Societe Generale, SMBC and Huntington are in the group.

This is not project debt tied to a building. It is platform money, the kind that pays interconnection deposits and vendor obligations ahead of occupancy. Rowan says it has raised more than $8 billion from over 40 lenders in 18 months, against a 5.5 GW pipeline. The letter-of-credit line matters most to anyone selling power equipment or utility interconnects: it is how a developer posts security without burning cash.

Lenders are underwriting speed to power, not the lease, and they will take a developer's balance sheet for the gap. The $600 million upsize takes that facility from $300 million to $900 million.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Rowan Digital Infrastructure · Site: n/d, multi-market platform · Footprint: n/d · Load: 5.5 GW pipeline · Facilities: $600M term loan upsize (Apterra lead), $590M revolver and $355M LC line (Citibank lead) · Deal value: $1.5B+ · Source: POWER.

Kraft Pitches 900 MW to a Town With No Zoning for It

The Kraft Group, through Unified Global Packaging Group, which owns the land, is proposing a data center campus on a 448-acre property in Sutton, Massachusetts, south of Worcester (Bisnow and Baxtel differ on the mileage, so I am not printing one). Up to 10 buildings of roughly 250,000 square feet each, 2.5 million square feet in total, and 900 MW at full build-out. The reported project cost is about $16.75 billion, with about $3.35 billion for phase one.

Divide it out and you get 360 W/sf on a gross basis, which is a moderate density for a campus billed as AI-class. No tenant is disclosed. Phase one is two buildings in 2029, then two a year through 2033. A 345 kV line crosses the property and the developer is in talks with ISO-NE, and treated sewage water is under study for cooling.

The approvals list is long: the Select Board, a zoning bylaw amendment (data centers are not currently a zoned use), a Town Meeting vote and a community benefits agreement. Residents packed a Select Board meeting this week. The tax pitch is the first two buildings producing about $10.4 million a year starting in fiscal 2031. Land with a transmission line through it is the asset here. Whether a Massachusetts town meeting will vote for the buildings is a separate question, and no date has been set.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Kraft Group / Unified Global Packaging Group · Site: Sutton, MA, 448-acre property, south of Worcester (mileage varies by source), inside Sutton town limits · Footprint: up to 2.5M sf, 10 buildings of ~250 ksf · Load: 900 MW (~360 W/sf gross at full build-out, my math) · Lease: n/d, no tenant disclosed · Deal value: ~$16.75B total, ~$3.35B phase one · Source: Bisnow, Baxtel.

Fluidstack Starts Digging in Wythe County

Smaller and quieter: Fluidstack has started construction on a 99-acre campus at Progress Park, Lot 35, in Wythe County, Virginia, east of Wytheville and well away from Loudoun. The project is reported at more than $1 billion, was announced under a different developer name in December 2025, and moved to Fluidstack this year. Appalachian Power serves it, with Fluidstack paying for substation upgrades. Cooling is closed loop with a one-time 400,000 gallon fill, and backup is three generators with no on-site gas generation.

Construction is expected to wrap mostly by the end of 2027, with about 1,000 jobs at peak and 75 permanent. The county expects roughly $10 million a year in revenue by 2028. There is no new lease or financing here, so I am filing it as a site signal and not a deal. The load, footprint and tenant are all undisclosed, and both sources I could open are secondary. Southwest Virginia is the second-tier pick of a developer that cannot get a megawatt in Northern Virginia on a useful timeline.

Deal specs. Sponsor: Fluidstack · Site: Progress Park Lot 35, Wythe County, VA, 99 acres, east of Wytheville · Footprint: n/d · Load: n/d · Lease: n/d, no tenant disclosed · Deal value: $1B+ · Source: Baxtel.

What the Week Adds Up To

Strip the logos off and the pattern is plain. Capital is paying up for assets that are already energized and leased (7.0% on AWS shells), and charging developers for the privilege of waiting on assets that are not (a $1.5 billion platform raise, a 20-year fixed charge on plants that do not exist yet). The spread between those two is the market's real price for time to power.

For brokers, the useful read is that a shell with a signed interconnect now sells on yield like a bond, while land with a line through it sells on a story. For developers, the Sutton filing is the reminder that the story still has to survive a town meeting. Zoning is the cheapest diligence item and the one most often skipped until the room fills up.

What to Watch Next Week

BXDC closing disclosures. The AWS portfolio is expected to close in the fourth quarter. An 8-K naming the campuses would give the first square footage and density for the 13 shells.

San Joaquin County, California. The Board's 45-day freeze on large data centers, voted 5 to 0 on October 7, runs out around November 21. An extension is expected, but no vote date is published.

Pennsylvania. The legislative session ends November 30. HB 2888 would require hyperscale developers to file community-benefit plans with at least $250 million of investment. It sits in the House Energy Committee with no vote scheduled.

Indianapolis. A council committee rejected $113 million of incentives for a DC BLOX campus. The full council votes December 7.

Sutton, Massachusetts. The Select Board, zoning bylaw and Town Meeting steps for the Kraft campus have no dates yet. Any posted agenda is the first real signal.
