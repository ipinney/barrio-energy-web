Tarrant Closes the Abatement Door and Cameron Will Not Open One for SpaceX

By Andi | Market Intelligence Analyst · NOVEMBER 20, 2026 · 6 min read
Share Post LinkedIn Email

Two Texas county courts voted on Tuesday, Nov 17, and neither vote was about whether a data center can be built. Both were about whether the public should help pay for one. Tarrant County shut abatements to the whole category, and Cameron County declined to even schedule a hearing on SpaceX's request. Meanwhile the state-level pause that was supposed to settle the argument has started to lift.

For anyone siting load in Texas, the fight is moving from "should it exist" to "who subsidizes it." That is a narrower fight, and one a developer can price if they see it coming.

Tarrant County Writes Data Centers Out of Its Abatements

On Nov 17 the Tarrant County Commissioners Court voted unanimously, with no commissioner speaking against it, on a resolution from Commissioner Matt Krause. It directs County Administrator Chandler Merritt to develop abatement guidelines that disqualify data centers over 1,000 square feet, battery storage over 5 MW, power generation over 5 MW, and the substations, cooling and transmission that go with them. Projects already under construction are not affected.

Read the list twice. The generation and storage thresholds are 5 MW, which reaches well past the hyperscale campuses that draw the headlines. A developer pairing a 50 MW load with on-site gas or a battery loses the abatement on both halves. Merritt still has to turn the resolution into guidelines, so the exact language is the thing to read when it appears.

The vote landed the same day the Texas Tribune published its tally of what local governments have given up. Its analysis found at least $1 billion in local property taxes forgone across 53 agreements at 34 data centers, in exchange for 465 guaranteed jobs. About half the agreements carried no job requirement at all, and the state is forgoing at least $3.2 billion in sales tax over two years. With those numbers in print, "no abatements" is an easy vote for a commissioner to cast.

Cameron County Says No to a Hearing on SpaceX

Also on Nov 17, the Cameron County Commissioners Court voted 3 to 2 against holding a public hearing on SpaceX's request for a 100% property tax abatement for 10 years on its Starbase data center, a project reported at $24 billion for the initial phases. Judge Eddie Trevino Jr. and Precinct 2 Commissioner Joey Lopez voted yes. Lopez said the vote was only about holding a hearing, and that without it the request is dead.

This updates the Starbase story from last week, when SpaceX's filing for 748 MW of gas generation was the news. The county is not the only door. The City of Starbase has its own hearing on Dec 9 covering a tax abatement and an economic development agreement, with terms not yet public.

The State Pause Lifts at TCEQ and Holds at ERCOT

Gov. Greg Abbott's office told TCEQ on Nov 13 that ERCOT had finished gathering community-impact information, which ends the environmental permit pause Abbott ordered in September. On Nov 16, TCEQ issued SpaceX's air permit for 35 natural gas turbines, about 750 MW, on a 444-acre tract, with nitrogen oxide emissions capped at 248 tons a year.

The grid side has not moved. ERCOT's full findings are due to the PUC on Dec 10, and large loads wait on that. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office in January and, per Fox, plans to reinstate a pause, so the permitting window that just reopened may be short. A developer with an application in the TCEQ queue should treat the next few weeks as worth more than the next few quarters.

Cottle County Approves 96 MW and Its Own Gas Plant

Not everyone is closing the window. On Monday, Nov 9, Cottle County commissioners voted 5 to 0 to create a reinvestment zone and grant an abatement for Aetheris's "Project Matter," a 96 MW data center paired with a 180 MW gas plant, about 15 miles east of Paducah. Aetheris leases up to 3,710 acres of Farmland Reserve land, which is owned by the LDS Church, and the zone covers 4,252 acres.

The terms are specific: $1.5 million to the county within 90 days, then $2.5 million a year, and at least 40 jobs. Set that against the Tribune's finding that half of Texas abatements carry no job requirement. A rural county with a small tax base and a developer who pays up front is a structure that can survive the current mood. The gas plant is nearly twice the size of the load, so the project is built around not waiting for the grid.

Red Oak Approves a Bigger Campus While a Suit Is Pending

In Ellis County, the Red Oak City Council approved, 4 to 1, an expansion of the Compass Datacenters campus to nearly 900 acres, over organized community objection. The original approval in May, at about 830 acres, is the subject of a lawsuit filed in September that has no ruling yet.

The council has approved a larger footprint before a court has decided whether it approved the smaller one properly. If the suit succeeds, the expansion inherits the defect. If it fails, opposition will have cost the developer nothing. Either way, a rezoning that survives a vote is not yet a rezoning that survives a judge.

NRG Sells About 2 GW of ERCOT Gas to LS Power

On Nov 9 NRG agreed to sell six gas plants totaling about 4.9 GW to LS Power for $4 billion in cash, with closing targeted for the second quarter of 2027. Three of the plants are in ERCOT: the Jack County and Johnson County combined-cycle units and R.W. Miller, together about 2 GW. The others are in New York and New England. NRG says it keeps roughly 10 GW in ERCOT after the sale.

For data center developers shopping for firm supply, an operating plant changing owners is a counterparty change to track. A new owner means a new negotiation, and closing is still two quarters out.

NRC Permits Four Reactors at Dow's Seadrift Site

The NRC approved the construction permit for Dow and X-energy's Long Mott project in Calhoun County on Nov 9. It covers four Xe-100 reactors at 80 MWe each, 320 MWe total, and is the first NRC construction permit for a high-temperature gas-cooled reactor in nearly 60 years. First power is not expected until the early 2030s.

Nothing here helps a load that wants power in 2028. It does show where the nuclear money is going: onto an industrial site with a single anchor customer, not onto the transmission system.

What to Watch Next Week

Tarrant guidelines. Merritt's draft decides how far the 5 MW thresholds reach. Look for it on a commissioners court agenda.

Dec 9, Starbase. The city's hearing on SpaceX's abatement and development agreement. It is the next point at which those terms become public.

Dec 10, ERCOT to the PUC. The community-impact findings are due, and the next large-load report is expected around then. It is when the queue number on the Grid Board gets refreshed.

Lubbock. A second public hearing on the city's 90-day moratorium is reportedly set for Dec 10, though I could not confirm that against the city agenda. Check before you plan around it.
